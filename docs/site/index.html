  1. 1

    Tool sprawl and re-keying score 12/12

    At stake: $1,000-$1,500 a month of software plus 2-8 office hours a week.

  2. 2

    Books live outside, and the sync breaks score 12/12

    Jobber's QuickBooks Online sync is one-way, with no Desktop support. A Real Green reviewer calls its accounting "basically unusable".

    At stake: Bookkeeper hours, wrong AR, a $38-$275 QuickBooks bill.

    Rootline: Built-in double-entry ledger · doc 09

  3. 3

    Locked-in processors and rate creep score 11/12

    "The company was bought by a credit card processor so you no longer have a choice of processor" (Service Autopilot review). Jobber charges 1% for instant payouts.

    At stake: 0.3-1.0 points of processing; 400 saved cards to re-collect on exit.

    Rootline: Bring-your-own Stripe, zero markup · doc 08

  4. 4

    Pricing for profit is hard score 11/12

    Jobber quotes are line items with no production-rate engine. LMN-style overhead math lives in a separate tool.

    At stake: 2-5 points of net margin on mispriced installs.

    Rootline: Budget-based estimating with overhead recovery · doc 03

  5. 5

    Hard to learn, costly to switch score 11/12

    Aspire reviewers describe an "intensive ramp up period". LMN charges $797-$1,497 to implement; Real Green about $995.

    At stake: Fees, and a lost spring if go-live slips.

    Rootline: Opinionated defaults; migration in days · doc 19, 21

  6. 6

    Crew apps crash or need signal score 10/12

    Legacy Aspire Crew Mobile averaged 2.28 stars and LMN reviewers report crashes. Spanish crew interfaces are unverified almost everywhere.

    At stake: Lost time records and unbilled extras.

    Rootline: Offline-first crew app in English and Spanish · doc 05

  7. 7

    Measuring is slow or a second subscription score 10/12

    Aspire sells PropertyIntel separately. One LMN reviewer says measuring a large commercial site "would take literal days".

    At stake: Estimator days per commercial bid.

    Rootline: Parcel and aerial measurement in the Design Studio · doc 10

  8. 8

    Routing that wastes drive time score 9/12

    Jobber's optimizer has used straight-line distance on 30-40 stop days.

    At stake: Drive time is unbilled labor, and labor is about 43% of revenue.

04 · THE SPINE

Lead to ledger, on one record

The spine of the product is the path one job takes. Every step is owned by a module doc and reads and writes the same record, so nothing is re-keyed. Numbers in the boxes are the docs that specify each step.

  1. Lead. Forms, the homeowner funnel, Google LSA, Angi and referrals land in one deduped inbox.
  2. AI design. The address becomes a site model; the designer draws to scale with region-rated plants and renders the client's yard.
  3. Estimate. Takeoff quantities priced with production rates, burdened labor and overhead recovery.
  4. Proposal. Good/Better/Best with mockups and plant cards, e-signature and a deposit in the portal.
  5. Job. One-click Work Order, Project or Service Agreement, gated on deposit, materials, 811 and permits.
  6. Visits. Recurrence, dispatch, routing and weather; crews finish visits offline with time, photos and chemical records.
  7. Invoice. Per visit, batched, level pay, milestone, progress or T&M, with per-line tax.
  8. Payment. Card, wallet, ACH or autopay as a direct charge on the Company's own Stripe account.
  9. Ledger. Posting rules create balanced entries; Stripe payouts match Plaid bank lines.
  10. Insights. Dashboards on one KPI Dictionary; actual production rates flow back into estimating.

Design Studio, AI mockups and the Plant KB: one plant-faithful loop

Jose draws the Hendersons' backyard to scale on the parcel and aerial. Every plant he can pick comes from the Central Ohio palette. The mockup renders that plan onto Dana's own photo, so the picture can only show plants that are on the plan, in the Price Book and proven in the Region.

  • Closed-world plants. Generators draw only plants the plan selected. Free text is mapped to KB candidates first, never straight to the image model.
  • Region-locked. Rating 3 or higher in the Plant Region Profile, conditions met, legal in the jurisdiction, with a render-ready reference pack.
  • Scale from measurement. Calibration card, dimension pins, parcel and aerial, depth with a visible confidence score.
  • Traceable and honest. Each rendered plant maps to a plan item and a Price Book line. A new Design Version marks old mockups stale. Every image carries an "Artist rendering" badge and C2PA credentials.

DOC 08

Bring-your-own Stripe: client money never touches Rootline

Each Company connects the Stripe account it owns, through OAuth or Account Links, as a Standard-style account configured with controller properties. Rootline creates direct charges on that account. The client pays Greenleaf, Stripe pays out to Greenleaf's bank, and Rootline only orchestrates and records.

  • Every common method. Cards, Apple Pay and Google Pay, ACH Direct Debit, and autopay with mandates and pre-notices.
  • Zero markup by default. The opt-in Pay-as-you-grow plan adds a disclosed 0.5% application fee, capped at $10 a payment.
  • Portable. A Company that leaves keeps its account, its saved cards and its history. A restricted-API-key mode is the advanced, secondary path.
  • Separate billing. Rootline's own subscriptions run on a separate platform Stripe account with Stripe Billing.
See the client pay drawer with autopay enrollment
DOC 09

A ledger that keeps itself, with QuickBooks optional

Every invoice, payment, payout, bill, timesheet and material issue posts a balanced, append-only journal entry through versioned posting rules, tagged by division, job, phase and property. The bookkeeper reviews instead of re-typing. QuickBooks Online can stay as a mirror for the CPA; Desktop and Xero follow in V1.

  • Plaid bank feeds and Stripe payouts proven against balance transactions, then matched to bank lines.
  • Job costing and deferred revenue come from the same entries, so the P&L by service line is ready on the 1st.
  • Nightly proofs. The trial balance nets to zero and each control account equals its subledger, or an integrity case opens.

11 · ARCHITECTURE

Architecture at a glance

A TypeScript modular monolith on Aurora PostgreSQL with PostGIS and pgvector, row-level security on every tenant table, and a few satellite services where the runtime differs. Field changes are never lost, client money is never held, and every model call goes through one AI Gateway. Spec: 20-architecture-security.md.

Tenancy

Shared schema with tenant_id on every table, forced row-level security, Cedar policies as defense in depth, and cross-tenant canaries in production.

Events and workflows

A transactional outbox feeds SNS and SQS. Temporal runs deposits, season-long agreement billing, snow events, mockup generation and Stripe event processing.

Offline sync

SQLite on the device and a bucket-based pull and push protocol Rootline owns. Field mutations are never refused for billing reasons.

Trust and security

PCI SAQ-A by design, SOC 2 Type I within 6 months of GA, a hash-chained audit log, and no training on customer data.
